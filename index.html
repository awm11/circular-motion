The motor ramps to the new speed at 4 rad s⁻². While it speeds up, friction must also supply a tangential force, so a coin near the limit can slip during spin-up.
The mass cancels in mω²r ≤ μsmg, so the critical speed doesn't depend on m. Try changing the mass while the coin is close to slipping.
Once slipping, kinetic friction
(μk < μs) acts against the coin's motion relative to the surface. It's too weak to hold a circle, so the coin spirals outwards and eventually leaves the table on a near-tangent.
